Transparent decision rules

A feasible and cheaper group purchase is an opportunity, never an automatic decision.

Rules used in this demonstration

  1. Only committed orders are considered; draft and withdrawn orders are excluded.
  2. Compatible demand must have exactly the same product/specification ID, order window, delivery area and required date. This is deliberately conservative: there is no automatic ±date tolerance or cross-area routing.
  3. The planner proposes one supplier offer or the individual procurement baseline for each batch. Capacity is shared by offer ID across all windows. Active reservations reduce it, including reservations in a batch's own window.
  4. Both reserved and released rows occupy their offer and window for new reservations. Only reserved rows consume capacity. A proposed purchase uses an offer with a free key at evaluation time.
  5. Required date must permit the supplier's lead time; the minimum expected fill rate is 95% and the supplier must serve the delivery area.
  6. The highest qualifying supplier price tier is used based on total committed compatible kg.
  7. Total delivered cost = (kg × tier price) + fixed delivery + (kg × variable logistics) + handling.
  8. Each retailer's baseline sums (kg × own purchase price/kg + own logistics cost) across its orders. The whole-cent cap rounds that sum down to cents.
  9. The plan minimises total delivered cost across all batches. A supplier quote must be strictly cheaper than the combined baseline and fit within the sum of the retailer caps. When the cheapest fixed batches compete for capacity, exact search covers up to 100,000 combinations, including baseline choices. Larger conflicts retain the individual baselines.
  10. The optional retailer-group mode can select subsets of compatible retailers, single-retailer supplier purchases and individual baselines. Each retailer's orders within a compatibility bucket stay together. If the search reaches a limit, the dashboard displays the fixed-batch plan with the reason for the fallback.
  11. Each selected group is priced as a separate purchase. Capacity proposals sharing an offer and window are combined in one total. Confirm the separate-purchase pricing with the supplier before booking.
  12. Proposed charges are proportional to the retailer caps. Leftover cents go to the largest fractional shares, with lower retailer IDs breaking ties. The proposed charges recover the quote total and stay within each retailer's cap. This calculation depends on reliable price and logistics evidence.
  13. Withdrawals are allowed while the window remains open. Later changes require manual intervention; capacity reservations must be reviewed by the coordinator.
  14. Separate versus coordinated fulfilment is evaluated using supplied trip and route-distance evidence. A coordinated trip is not automatically assumed cheaper or better.

These are documented project decisions to be validated with the participating retailers and suppliers, not verified real-world operational rules.
