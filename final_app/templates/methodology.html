Transparent decision rules

A feasible and cheaper group purchase is an opportunity, never an automatic decision.

Rules used in this demonstration

  1. Only committed orders are considered; draft and withdrawn orders are excluded.
  2. Compatible demand must have exactly the same product/specification ID, order window, delivery area and required date. This is deliberately conservative: there is no automatic ±date tolerance or cross-area routing.
  3. One supplier must be able to supply the entire batch; no supplier splitting is assumed. Available capacity is reduced by previously reserved allocations.
  4. Required date must permit the supplier's lead time; the minimum expected fill rate is 95% and the supplier must serve the delivery area.
  5. The highest qualifying supplier price tier is used based on total committed compatible kg.
  6. Total delivered cost = (kg × tier price) + fixed delivery + (kg × variable logistics) + handling.
  7. Individual baseline = sum for each retailer of (kg × own purchase price/kg + own logistics cost).
  8. Accept aggregation only when a feasible group option is strictly cheaper than the baseline. Equal-cost, higher-cost or infeasible choices are rejected.
  9. Shared group cost is allocated proportional to retailer kg; final retailer receives the cents rounding adjustment.
  10. Withdrawals are allowed while the window remains open. Later changes require manual intervention; capacity reservations must be reviewed by the coordinator.
  11. Separate versus coordinated fulfilment is evaluated using supplied trip and route-distance evidence. A coordinated trip is not automatically assumed cheaper or better.

These are documented project decisions to be validated with the participating retailers and suppliers, not verified real-world operational rules.
